# OpenRouter Market Movement: 2026-07-09 to 2026-07-23

This note applies the interpretation contract in [`schema/model_activity.schema.json`](../schema/model_activity.schema.json) to the local weekly archive.

## Method

Whole-market comparisons use only dates with at least 300 `model_activity` rows. Provider totals combine all observed route variants and group by the namespace before `/`. Total tokens means prompt plus completion tokens; reasoning and cached counters are not added again.

The newest date, 2026-07-24, is incomplete and excluded. The 2026-07-02 snapshot is also excluded because a migration test refreshed prompt/completion/count for its Top-20 rows while the long tail retained the original batch vintage. The reproducible baseline therefore starts at 2026-07-09.

Reasoning, cached, and tool metrics use only rows where `activity_coverage.supplement_seen=1`. The comparison uses a fixed cohort present on all six days in both 2026-07-10..15 and 2026-07-17..22.

## Market Growth

| Date | Routes | Tokens | Requests | Tokens/request |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2026-07-09 | 421 | 7.769T | 520.8M | 14,919 |
| 2026-07-16 | 421 | 9.619T | 573.0M | 16,787 |
| 2026-07-23 | 433 | 8.752T | 581.6M | 15,048 |

From July 9 to July 23, tokens grew 12.7% and requests grew 11.7%. Tokens per request changed by less than 1%. The July 16 peak was not a new stable baseline: free-route traffic, especially Tencent HY3, created a temporary long-context pulse that normalized the following week.

Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 is the clearest token winner. It alone represented 18.24% of all July 23 route tokens. StepFun 3.7 Flash and Kimi K3 were the fastest smaller-base growers. Kimi K3 reached 186.5B tokens, or 2.13% of the market snapshot, within its first observed week.

Anthropic lost both absolute volume and share. MiniMax fell more sharply. Tencent needs careful interpretation: traffic moved between the old preview slug, the new HY3 slug, and a temporary free route. Comparing one slug or one variant would overstate both the preceding surge and subsequent collapse.

## Two Different Markets

Token share and request share describe different workloads. On July 23, Xiaomi led tokens with 19.27% but had only 4.29% of requests. Google had 8.66% of tokens but 27.94% of requests. DeepSeek occupied the middle with 16.50% of tokens and 20.70% of requests.

This is evidence of a split between high-frequency shorter requests and lower-frequency long-context workloads. It is not evidence about revenue, users, latency, or model quality.

## Telemetry Shift

| Fixed-cohort metric | Jul 10..15 | Jul 17..22 | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Reasoning / completion | 44.62% | 39.04% | -5.58pp |
| Cached / prompt | 77.59% | 78.41% | +0.82pp |
| Tool calls / 100 requests | 25.51 | 24.45 | -4.2% |
| Requests with tool error | 0.602% | 0.522% | -0.08pp |
| Tokens / request | 21,603 | 21,070 | -2.5% |

Reasoning intensity declined across the fixed Top-model cohort, while cache use was essentially flat. Tool-call density also declined slightly, but the share of requests containing a tool error improved. These counters depend on provider telemetry conventions, so they support within-model and fixed-cohort trend analysis more strongly than cross-provider level comparisons.

## Interpretation Limits

OpenRouter traffic is a proxy for routed workload, not the full LLM market. Provider grouping uses publisher namespaces rather than inference hosts. New route slugs can represent migrations rather than net-new demand. Free variants can create large temporary pulses. Dates with fewer than 300 rows are changing Top-N panels and must not be summed as whole-market volume.
